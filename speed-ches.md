    <h1>Party positions on a single event, measured quickly.</h1>
    <p class="lede">Short surveys run alongside the main CHES waves, each asking experts about party positioning on one specific development.</p>
  </div>

<section>
  <div class="wrap split">
    <div><p class="label">About the series</p></div>
    <div class="prose">
      <p>Each SPEED survey asks a panel of political scientists specialising in political parties and European integration about party positions on a single issue, alongside general ideological positions, so the results can be read against the main <a href="{{ '/ches-europe/' | relative_url }}">CHES-Europe</a> waves.</p>

      <div class="note">
        <p><strong>Two things I need from you here.</strong> First, what SPEED stands for &mdash; I've left it as the bare acronym rather than guess at an expansion.</p>
        <p>Second, this intro is written only from what the two dataset descriptions below already say. If there's a fuller account of the series on Squarespace or in one of the papers, send it over and I'll replace this paragraph.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

      <div class="dataset">
        <h3>SPEED CHES &mdash; Ukraine (2023)</h3>
        <p class="dataset-meta">Fielded June 2023 &middot; 217 experts &middot; 274 parties &middot; 30 countries</p>
        <p>Administered in June 2023 and completed by 217 political scientists specializing in political parties and European integration, this survey provides information about the positioning of 274 parties on policies related to the Russian invasion of Ukraine, along with general ideological positions. The survey covers political parties in 30 countries, including all European Union member states except Luxembourg and Cyprus, plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Turkey, and the United Kingdom.</p>
