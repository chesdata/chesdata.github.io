    <p class="label">CHES-Europe</p>
    <h1>25+ years of party position data.</h1>
    <p class="lede">Expert placements of party positions on general left-right, economic left-right, GAL-TAN, and European integration are common to every wave, with specific issue positions available since 1999.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section>
  <div class="wrap split">
    <div><p class="label">About the series</p></div>
    <div class="prose">
      <p>Questions on parties' general position on European integration, several EU policies, general left-right, economic left-right, and GAL-TAN (Green/Alternative/Libertarian-Tradition/Authoritarian/Nationalist) are common to all surveys. More recent surveys also contain questions on non-EU policy issues, such as anti-elite rhetoric, immigration, redistribution, decentralization, and environmental policy. More information about the survey and the trend file can be found in the codebook.</p>

      <p>The number of countries increased from 14 Western European countries in 1999 to 24 current or prospective EU members in 2006 to 31 countries in 2024. In this time, the number of national parties grew from 143 to 279. The 1999&ndash;2024 trend survey includes all EU member states except Luxembourg, plus the United Kingdom.</p>

    </div>
  </div>
</section>

        <h3>2024 Chapel Hill Expert Survey</h3>
        <p class="dataset-meta">Fielded Oct&ndash;Dec 2024 &middot; 609 experts &middot; 279 parties &middot; 31 countries</p>
        <p>CHES was administered between October 10 and November 20, 2024 for EU-West countries plus Turkey, and between November 1 and December 17, 2024 for EU-East countries. The survey was completed by 609 political scientists specializing in political parties and European integration. This wave provides information about the positioning of the leadership of 279 parties in 31 countries on ideology, policy, populism, democracy, European integration, and select EU policy questions. Country coverage includes all EU member states except Luxembourg, plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Turkey, and the United Kingdom.</p>
